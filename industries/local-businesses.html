What we see with local businesses

Three situations that keep coming up

Owners opening a first or second location tend to face the same three problems. None of them is about the product. They're about who is nearby and whether those people can find you.

The lease is signed on a feeling

The unit looked busy on one Saturday. Nobody checked who lives within a ten-minute walk, where the transit stops are, or why the last tenant left. Five years is a long time to be wrong.

The website exists and does nothing

A page a cousin built in 2019, slow on a phone, no clear list of services, no way to book. It doesn't show up for "near me" searches, so it doesn't bring anyone in.

The map listing is half filled in

Wrong hours, no photos, three reviews from two years ago. The competitor two blocks over has forty reviews and appears first when someone nearby searches.

Questions we get on the first call

Local businesses, before you book

We haven't signed the lease yet. Is it too early to talk?

It's the best time. Location research is a two-week sprint and it's cheapest before you're committed. You get a shortlist with the numbers behind each option and a plain recommendation.

We already have a website. Do we need a new one?

Not always. A one-week website audit scores what you have across speed, search, and accessibility, and ranks the fixes. Sometimes a fix list is all you need; sometimes the honest answer is to rebuild.

Do you sell online stores or run ads?

No to both. We build service and brochure sites, not e-commerce, and we don't run paid ads. If you need either we'll say so on the first call and point you to someone who does that well.

How fast will we show up on the map?

Setup takes two weeks. Google usually reflects a verified, complete profile within days; ranking against established competitors depends on reviews and consistency, which is why the setup includes a review routine you can keep running.
