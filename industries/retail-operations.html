What we see in this industry

Three situations that keep coming up

Retail chains with a few stores and a few dozen staff tend to have the same three problems. None of them is a technology problem at first. They're rules nobody wrote down.

The schedule is one person's memory

One manager knows who can open, who can't close, and what the collective agreement says about breaks. When they're on holiday, the grievances start.

Audits happen on paper, then vanish

Store checks get done on a clipboard or a phone photo. Nobody can say which stores failed the same item three months running, so nothing changes.

The new location is a guess

The lease is signed on gut feel and foot traffic from one Saturday. The demographics of the block, who lives there and who commutes through, never made it into the decision.

Questions we get on the first call

Retail operations, before you book

Our rules are in a union agreement. Can you work from that?

Yes, and we prefer it. ShiftFlow's scheduling and break rules were encoded from the collective agreement rather than from what managers remembered. The agreement becomes the acceptance criteria, which is why the tool doesn't generate grievances.

We're a small chain. Is this overkill?

ShiftFlow schedules about 19 people. The problems on this page show up at that size because there's no ops department to absorb them. A two-week sprint to write the rules down is the smallest useful step.

Do you build the scheduling or audit tool yourselves?

We write the brief, run the prototype and own the backlog. The production build goes to your developer or a vendor we help you pick, with a spec they can quote. ShiftFlow and Scrutas both ran that way.

Can you help us pick a store location?

Yes. Location and demography research is a two-week sprint using public data on who lives, works and commutes around a site. It's the newest service, so the first case study is being written from the first engagement.
